U.S. Ecommerce
Homepage Refresh

A focused U.S. ecommerce refresh ahead of a broader rebrand, supported by competitive research, UX recommendations and Figma preparation for development.

ClientLOWA
My roleEcommerce UX · Research · Figma
Year2026
ToolsShopify Plus · Figma · Figma MCP · Claude · Semrush · Firefly
ResultCheckouts from homepage sessions up 44% in the first five weeks after launch
01 / Context

The challenge

LOWA needed to improve its U.S. Shopify Plus homepage without investing in a complete redesign ahead of a larger brand overhaul. The project had a firm September deadline and a limited budget.

The existing homepage relied on lifestyle imagery and promotional tiles linking to individual boots. The opportunity was to create clearer ways into the assortment, particularly for visitors unfamiliar with the brand, while strengthening the page’s search foundations.

02 / Contribution

Research into a
build-ready file.

LOWA’s team developed the visual concept. I contributed competitive research, shopping-path recommendations, and a search and AI-visibility audit, then translated the team’s HTML mockup into desktop and mobile Figma files for the developers.

01 / RESEARCH

Define shopping paths

Through kickoff questions and a review of about twelve competitor homepages, I recommended shopping by activity, fit needs, new arrivals and product collections.

02 / SEARCH AND AI

Improve findability

My audit found that AI answers already ranked LOWA first for durability and ankle support. I recommended clearer headings, metadata and structured data so search engines and AI tools could find that strength on LOWA’s own site.

03 / FIGMA

Make it buildable

Using Claude and the Figma MCP, I prepared desktop and mobile layouts and reviewed typography, responsive behavior, product imagery, links and Shopify-driven content. I also flagged mismatches between visuals and shopping labels, including backpacking imagery paired with hiking calls to action. LOWA’s team discussed the feedback with me and revised imagery and other details.

4

The shift

Four ways into the assortment, where there was one.

  • Shop by activity
  • Shop by need
  • New arrivals
  • Collections
03 / Ecommerce UX

From promotions
to product discovery.

The earlier page led from large lifestyle and product tiles to individual products. The refreshed structure offered multiple entry points: activity, waterproof or wide-fit needs, new arrivals and product collections. Visitors could browse relevant options before deciding on a specific boot.

My handoff addressed the details that affect how a design behaves once it reaches Shopify: available font weights, product images served on white, clearer CTA labels, and which sections pull products from the store. On desktop, the hero crossfades between photos, without the flash of background in the supplied code; phones get one still image.

I also recommended accessible heading structure, keyboard focus states, carousel behavior and badge contrast improvements. And I started a small brand system in Figma with Claude and the Figma MCP, drawn from what LOWA’s German and U.S. sites already used, ahead of the upcoming brand change.

04 / Outcome

A timely launch.
Clearer shopping paths.

The page launched within budget for September. In the first five weeks, checkouts from sessions that began on the homepage increased 44% while homepage visits were about 10% lower. Desktop clicks per homepage view increased approximately 28%. On phones, “Shop Men’s Hiking,” one of the solid white hero buttons from my file, became the second most-tapped element.

The release also included an H1, a page description and headings for each section. The work reinforced the importance of designing for the actual storefront, from loaded fonts and product photography to responsive behavior and live merchandising data.

Five-week observed results from LOWA’s analysis; not an attribution of store-wide sales or a causal claim about the refresh alone.

Also for LOWA: At LOWA’s request, I tested AI image-to-video tools (Higgsfield, Kling, Seedance, Magnific and DomoAI) for 360° product rotations, as a lower-cost alternative to a studio shoot for product pages and social. The U.S. team reviewed them closely against the real boot and sent a couple to headquarters in Germany for review.
