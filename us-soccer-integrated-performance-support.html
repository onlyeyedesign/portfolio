01 / Context

The challenge

IPS needed a consistent way to communicate its evolving purpose, capabilities and approach. Existing information was spread across presentations and working documents, while the division also needed a place within U.S. Soccer’s established website.

The work called for clarity across different applications, without inventing a separate visual language or introducing unnecessary development requirements.

02 / Contribution

Existing standards.
Purpose-built solutions.

I worked with IPS leadership and subject-matter experts to turn changing requirements into an identity, visual model, reusable design guidance and a practical structure for its digital presence.

01 / VISUAL LANGUAGE

Identity and model

Developed IPS-specific lockups from the U.S. Soccer crest and designed the visual model, refining its structure as the division’s terminology evolved. The model’s white labels pass WCAG AA contrast.

02 / REUSABLE GUIDANCE

AI-enabled design system

Consolidated existing U.S. Soccer brand standards and IPS-specific assets into an AI-enabled design system packaged as a Claude skill. Team members can request presentations and other branded materials in everyday language, with the system drawing on the established guidelines.

03 / DIGITAL EXPERIENCE

Site architecture and wireframes

Structured IPS content and explored two wireframe treatments built around U.S. Soccer’s existing components and website patterns.

03 / Digital

Structure before styling.

I recommended a hub with a dedicated page for each performance domain. U.S. Soccer’s web team preferred a single scrolling page, and IPS leadership chose the hub. I refined the information architecture as requirements evolved, working within the existing Participate section. By late September, seven menu pages had become five: About IPS and Approach merged into one page, and Affiliations became a section of the National Training Center page. The National Training Center already has a main page on ussoccer.com for visitors, donors, media and event bookings, so the IPS page covers the performance environment, and the two pages link to each other.

I explored two wireframe directions using established site patterns: a content-focused Standard layout and a more visually expressive Enhanced layout, both intended to support efficient implementation by the internal web team. My wireframes specified one H1 per page, a descriptive link for each domain card, and the domains and outcomes as live text, not only within the IPS model image.

04 / Outcome

A clearer foundation.

A senior IPS leader independently created a presentation using the AI-enabled design system, without needing additional guidance. The IPS identity and visual model also established a consistent reference for the division’s communications.

IPS approved the proposed site map and Standard/Enhanced wireframes, which are now with U.S. Soccer’s web team. The project reinforced that some of the most important decisions happen before design: defining the language, organizing the content and asking the right questions.
